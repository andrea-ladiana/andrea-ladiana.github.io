A fractal that mostly plays one note

Strike a hierarchical lattice and it rings. Most of its sound sits on a single frequency; the rest climbs a ladder that can be written down exactly.

Based on A. Ladiana, The Exact Laplacian Spectrum of the Berker–Ostlund Diamond Lattice, J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. (2026).

Grow

Start from one edge. Replace it with b parallel two-step paths. Do the same to every new edge, again and again.

Distances double at each step while edges multiply by 2b, so the lattice has a fractal dimension between a line and a crowd. The two roots get busier and busier: their degree is bL.

03

Trapped

Why so much at λ = 2? Push two middle vertices of one diamond in opposite directions. Nothing else has to move.

click a diamond
3
diamond modes – + loop modes – = modes at λ = 2 –

Each diamond traps b − 1 such vibrations. Each independent loop one level down traps one more: flip the sign as you go around it. Count both and you get the multiplicity of λ = 2 exactly, at every size.

Zoom out

Erase the newest vertices and the lattice looks the same, only one level smaller. To keep the same vibration, the note has to move. Follow it.

Each zoom-out is the same two-variable map Φ. A note is a λ whose point lands on the dashed line after some number of zoom-outs; once there it falls to (−1, 0) and then sits at (1, 0) forever.

05

The ladder

Every note sits on a rung. Rung k holds the notes that need k zoom-outs: twice as many as the rung below, each b times quieter.

fraction of all modes below λ
3

In the infinite lattice the spectrum is made of notes only, no smear. All the weight is on the ladder, and the bottom rung alone takes (b − 1)/b.

Beyond the paper

Add a magnetic field

Thread a flux through every loop and the hopping picks up a phase. On square lattices this draws Hofstadter’s butterfly. What does the diamond draw?

Add disorder

Random weights break the exact self-similarity. Do the trapped modes survive, and how wide does each note become? Open questions from §6.
